# Design Brief: Public MVP Presentation

## Job And Audience

Audience: engineers and technical evaluators arriving at the public demo to understand whether the tool is real, scoped honestly, and worth opening.

Visitor mode: Persuade, restrained. The hero should introduce the engineering product, not become the product.

## Outcome And Proof

Primary outcome: the visitor opens the workbench with a correct expectation of what the MVP can and cannot do.

Proof to preserve:

- real product-derived 3D hull render;
- 2D + 3D capability;
- two geometry modes;
- equipment layout checks;
- JSON/SVG/CSV/theoretical drawing exports;
- explicit non-claim that this is not full CAD/CAE or validated full hydrostatics.

## Selected Direction

Keep the current dark abyssal engineering hero as the restrained brand surface. Simplify proof hierarchy: one product claim, one real-render proof block, one capability strip, and one honest limitation note.

Do not introduce generic marketing cards, fake CAD chrome, testimonials, or unsupported performance claims.

## Scope And Boundaries

Brief only; no implementation in this phase.

Preserve `Underwater Vehicle Designer` identity, Russian hero copy, real render source, and workbench entry path. If a future plan replaces the static render with a real 3D hero, it must remain product-derived and non-decorative.

## States And Ranges

Minimum: desktop hero, mobile hero, workbench jump, visualization jump.

Typical: real render visible with capability proof and limitations.

Maximum: future real 3D hero with fallback static render and performance guard.

## Interaction And Layout

Desktop: hero copy and render stay paired. Mobile: compress proof before the tool; avoid forcing a long marketing page before controls.

CTA sequence: open workbench first, inspect visualization second. Keep the public hero secondary to the product.

## Constraints And Open Decisions

Owner must decide whether to keep a static real render or plan a real 3D hero. Typography direction is also owner-controlled.
